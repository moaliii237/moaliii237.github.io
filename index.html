Roadside computer vision pilot

Solo, freelance

A paid pilot for a Dutch geodata company. From survey-van camera frames I detect fixed roadside obstacles and map the verge vegetation, then export both as GeoJSON layers for the client's online map, with a GPS position interpolated for every frame (this removed up to 87 m of map error). I wrote the annotation guidelines, labelled the data myself, and split train and test by GPS location, so the test streets were never seen in training. All three milestones were accepted.

When two checks of the model disagreed, I reviewed every detection scored as wrong and found 46 real objects missing from the test labels; with the labels corrected, precision at the working threshold was 75%, in line with a manual check of 112 live detections.

Obstacle detection went from 0.113 to 0.361 AP50 on the same classes, 3.2 times the client's existing model (tree trunks 0.125 to 0.821). Vegetation went from 0.438 to 0.818 mIoU. All on test streets held out by GPS location.
PyTorchRF-DETRDINOv2OpenCVGeoJSONROS2

Dutch speech-to-text for sales calls

Solo, freelance

A speech-to-text pipeline I built freelance for a Dutch energy-sales call center: Whisper large-v3-turbo, pyannote speaker diarization, an LLM clean-up step that may change only a limited part of each line, and an evaluation harness (word error rate, compliance-keyword F1, bootstrap confidence intervals) scored against hand-transcribed calls.

With Gemini (a cloud model) as the clean-up step: 88.2% F1 on compliance keywords (114 keyword mentions in 2 hand-transcribed calls), zero invented text and a 33.6% normalized word error rate.

The client wanted call data kept in-house, so I benchmarked 5 local open-weight LLMs against Gemini in 28 scored runs and built the final correction step on Qwen 2.5 7B, which fixes misheard names, suppliers and amounts using the customer's record from the client's API. I did not measure F1 for this local step. An earlier version used fine-tuned Dutch wav2vec2 models.

Root segmentation research

Solo

A controlled study of how the way you cut an image into patches changes both segmentation quality and the actual measurement of root length. I trained a ResUNet with 10 patch-size and overlap settings and compared them on the 17-image validation set.

512 px patches cut root-length error from 24.3% to 14.5% compared with 128 px and ran about 9 times faster. The setting with the best Dice score was not the one that measured length best.
